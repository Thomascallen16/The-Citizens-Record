Citizen Toolkit

Put It to Use

Practical, lawful steps for engaging with your government — contacting representatives, tracking legislation, and requesting public records.

Toolkit 01

Contact Your Representatives

Find your officials

Look up your representative, senators, and state legislators by address.

Pick the right channel

Phone calls and in-person meetings generally get more attention than form emails.

Reference the bill number

Cite the specific bill or issue — staff track constituent contact by topic.

Be specific and brief

State your position and the outcome you want in the first two sentences.

Toolkit 02

Track Legislation

Start at Congress.gov

Search by keyword, sponsor, or bill number to find full text and status.

Set up alerts

Congress.gov lets you save searches and follow specific bills for updates.

Read the committee report

Committee reports explain intent and often matter more than the bill text alone.

Check your state legislature

Most states have an equivalent public bill-tracking site — bookmark yours.

Toolkit 03

File a FOIA Request

Identify the right agency

FOIA covers federal agencies only — states have their own public records laws.

Write a specific request

Vague requests get delayed. Name the records, date range, and format you want.

Submit and log it

Most agencies have an online portal. Save your confirmation and tracking number.

Know your appeal rights

A denial isn't final — administrative appeals have their own short deadlines.

Toolkit 04

Public Outreach to Representatives

Town halls

Public meetings are recorded and often livestreamed — find the schedule.

Public comment periods

Agencies must accept and consider comments on proposed rules — this is your formal input.

Written testimony

Committees often accept written testimony from the public on pending bills.

Coalition letters

Organized, signed letters from constituent groups carry more institutional weight.
