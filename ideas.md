# The Citizen's Record — Design Direction

## Three possible approaches

### Approach 1
**Theme Name:** Civic Archive

**Very Brief Intro:** A civic publication that feels like an annotated municipal archive: sober typography, evidence tabs, and richly structured document surfaces. It makes verification feel tangible rather than academic.

**Probability:** 0.07

### Approach 2
**Theme Name:** Courtroom Margin

**Very Brief Intro:** A formal, high-contrast legal-journal aesthetic built around black rules, editorial red, and deliberate, generous whitespace. It creates gravity through restraint rather than ornamental patriotism.

**Probability:** 0.04

### Approach 3
**Theme Name:** Neighborhood Press

**Very Brief Intro:** A warm independent-publication style inspired by local print desks, field notebooks, and community bulletin boards. It makes civic learning approachable while preserving an exacting source-first posture.

**Probability:** 0.09

## Chosen approach: Civic Archive

### Design Movement

Civic Archive combines **contemporary editorial design** with the visual language of archived public records. It treats the site as a transparent reading room: physical in material, exact in hierarchy, and quietly modern in interaction. Its atmosphere is one of shared civic inheritance—not nostalgia, nationalism, or partisan performance.

### Core Principles

1. **Proof is visible.** Source links, dates, verification stamps, and document identifiers are always designed as first-class information.
2. **The reading path has cadence.** Dense factual material alternates with breathable margins, short explanatory labels, and purposeful rules.
3. **Archive, not institution.** The site is authoritative without impersonating a government website; it is visibly an independent civic-education publication.
4. **Clarity earns attention.** Navigation, interface labels, and motion make source exploration easier rather than more dramatic.

### Color Philosophy

The palette is built from **ink, paper, and signal color**. Midnight blue-black conveys the seriousness of a record room; mineral paper is warm enough to make long reading inviting; vermilion is reserved for action, provenance, and verification, so it never becomes decorative noise. Secondary moss indicates stable/evergreen public materials.

### Layout Paradigm

The page is organized as an **editorial archive** rather than a centered marketing funnel. A narrow left rail carries navigation cues and publication metadata; the principal reading column shifts between full-bleed dark record headers, source indexes, and offset explanatory blocks. The home page uses asymmetric two-column compositions and document-index rhythms rather than generic grids.

### Signature Elements

1. A **record locator**: monospace labels formatted like `RECORD / 001`, used for cards, sources, and section introductions.
2. A **source tab**: small vermilion or moss corner tabs that mark items as verified, reading, or pending review.
3. **Document rules**: hairline dividers and subtle baseline texture that structure content without clutter.

### Interaction Philosophy

Interactions should feel like retrieving and annotating a record. Links underline from the left, cards lift only slightly, and source rows reveal their primary-source context on hover or focus. Navigation on mobile uses an explicit, keyboard-accessible disclosure rather than an ambiguous icon-only control.

### Animation

Motion is brief, physical, and subordinate to reading. On page entry, only the hero metadata, headline, and source panel fade upward in a 30–60ms sequence. Source rows and cards use a 160ms transform/opacity transition. The design honors reduced-motion settings by making all transitions instantaneous.

### Typography System

**DM Serif Display** carries public-facing titles and quotations with a measured, editorial voice. **Manrope** handles paragraph copy, navigation, and controls for plain-language readability. **IBM Plex Mono** identifies records, dates, source types, and metadata. Headlines use a deliberate contrast of compact eyebrow labels, large serif statements, and unitalicized emphasis; body copy stays left-aligned and comfortably narrow.

### Brand Essence

**The Citizen's Record is a source-first civic reading room for people who want to see the public record before they hear the argument.**

Personality: **exact, independent, invitational**.

### Brand Voice

Headlines are direct and declarative. CTAs invite inspection rather than demand conversion. Microcopy explains what a reader will receive and what the site has not yet verified.

Example headline: “Read the document. Then decide what it means.”

Example CTA: “Open the source record”

### Wordmark & Logo

The mark is an abstract **document aperture**: three staggered file corners frame a small central evidence point. It contains no text, reads at favicon scale, and pairs with a custom-spaced serif wordmark in the header.

### Signature Brand Color

**Record Vermilion — #C64B35**

## Style Decisions

- Use full-width dark headers only for major reading transitions; keep the main body on warm paper to support long-form reading.
- Avoid faux-live statistics, fabricated legislative records, ratings, testimonials, and unsupported claims. Demonstration content must be evergreen, clearly framed, or linked to an official source.
- The newsletter is presented as “Coming soon,” not as a working subscription form.
- The contact action opens an email client and states this clearly; no promises of secure or confidential tip handling are made.
- Civic heritage is expressed through public documents, courthouse material, shared civic language, and dignity—not flags, party colors, military imagery, or faux-government seal language.
- The homepage gives people direct routes to the Constitution Annotated, Congress.gov, Regulations.gov, and official court opinions; it never presents those external records as legal advice or individual legal help.
- The primary content flow and footer state that the site is an educational reference only, does not provide legal advice or representation, and cannot assess a visitor's specific case.

## Editorial Charter and Field-Manual Model

### Core Principle

**Evidence Before Opinion.** The Citizen's Record does not ask readers to trust its conclusions. It teaches them where public information comes from, how systems create it, and how to examine it independently.

### Guiding Distinction

> Do not create followers of information. Create investigators of information.

The public experience should give visitors enough context to orient themselves without replacing a primary source with a summary. A resource always distinguishes **what happened**, **where the original source is**, **how the relevant system works**, **questions a citizen can ask**, and **how to verify the information independently**.

### Citizen Toolkit Structure

The Citizen Toolkit is not a second homepage feed. It is a carefully ordered field-manual section, reached from the main navigation and previewed on the homepage. Its five opening tools are: finding government documents, reading a docket, making a public-records request, following public money, and verifying a claim. Each tool gives a short method and routes readers to an official source or public research portal.

### Editorial Tone

The site speaks with calm confidence and respect for disagreement. It avoids activist language, partisan framing, sensationalism, legal conclusions, and claims that a short summary substitutes for a document. It helps a visitor become more capable—not more dependent—after every visit.
