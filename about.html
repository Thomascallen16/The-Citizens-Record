About

Why This Exists

The Citizen's Record is a civic education project, not a news outlet or an advocacy organization.

Mission

Most Americans learn about the law secondhand — through headlines built for clicks and campaign ads built for persuasion. The Citizen's Record goes to the primary source instead: the bill text, the court opinion, the agency filing, the public record. We explain what these documents actually say, in plain language, so you can form your own view instead of borrowing one.

The law belongs to the people. Understanding it shouldn't require a law degree.

Editorial Standards

  • Every factual claim traces back to a named, linkable primary source — a bill, an opinion, a transcript, a filing.
  • Analysis is clearly separated from summary. We label opinion as opinion.
  • We do not endorse candidates, parties, or ballot measures.
  • Corrections are posted openly, with the original error noted rather than quietly edited away.

Fact-Checking Policy

Before publication, every piece is checked against the underlying document it describes — not against a secondary summary of it. Where a claim is disputed or unsettled, we say so and show both readings rather than picking one.

Sources Policy

We prioritize official government sources: Congress.gov, agency rulemaking dockets, court systems' own opinion archives, and public filings. Where we rely on reporting or secondary analysis, it is credited by name and treated as a lead to verify — not as a source of record.

Educational Disclaimer

The Citizen's Record is an educational resource. Nothing on this site is legal advice, and reading it does not create an attorney-client relationship. If you have a specific legal problem, consult a licensed attorney in your jurisdiction.
