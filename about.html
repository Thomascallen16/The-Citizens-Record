Standards

Why This Exists

The Citizen's Record is a civic education and public-evidence project—not a news outlet, advocacy organization, law firm, or substitute for an attorney.

Mission

Most people encounter public institutions through secondhand explanations—headlines, arguments, summaries, or someone else's conclusion. The Citizen's Record goes to the underlying source instead: the bill text, court opinion, agency filing, public record, dataset, order, or other identifiable document. We help people organize what the record says so they can form their own view.

The goal is not to tell people what to believe. The goal is to make important claims easier to check.

Accountability Charter

We are building accountability instruments, not conclusion machines. The same standard we apply to public institutions must apply to the citizen, the source, the software, and ourselves.

  • Source before conclusion. Substantive assertions should trace to an identifiable source whenever one is available.
  • Fact is not inference. We distinguish what a document says from what someone believes it means.
  • Evidence cuts both ways. Evidence that contradicts a user's position belongs in the record too.
  • Uncertainty stays visible. We use UNKNOWN when the available record does not answer the question.
  • Conflicts stay visible. When sources disagree, we identify the conflict rather than quietly selecting the more convenient version.
  • Corrections remain traceable. Material corrections should be documented rather than silently rewritten.
  • Provenance matters. A useful record should make it possible to identify where information came from and, where practical, how it was derived.
  • The instrument is not the authority. The site does not replace the primary source, a court, an agency, or a licensed attorney.

Our governing question is simple: Can this claim be verified? If the answer is not yet yes, the record should make that limitation clear.

Truth Standard

These labels prevent different kinds of statements from collapsing into one another:

  • FACT — verified by a reliable source.
  • LAW — supported by an identified legal authority.
  • CLAIM — attributed to a person or organization.
  • QUESTION — specific and unresolved.
  • INFERENCE — a reasoned interpretation, not a direct source statement.
  • OPINION — a normative judgment.
  • UNKNOWN — the available record does not answer it yet.

Editorial Standards

  • Every factual claim should trace back to a named, linkable source whenever one exists.
  • Primary sources are preferred. Secondary sources are treated as leads to verify, not automatically as the source of record.
  • Analysis is clearly separated from summary. Opinion is labeled as opinion.
  • We do not endorse candidates, parties, or ballot measures.
  • We do not intentionally design the system to assume either that the citizen or the institution is correct.
  • Corrections are posted openly, with the material change explained when practical.

Fact-Checking Policy

Before publication, factual descriptions are checked against the underlying document or other identified source—not merely against a secondary summary. Where a claim is disputed or unsettled, we identify the competing accounts and the limits of the available evidence.

Sources Policy

We prioritize official government sources: legislative records, agency rulemaking dockets, court systems' opinion archives, public filings, official datasets, meeting records, and other original documents. Where reporting or secondary analysis is useful, it is credited by name and treated as a lead to verify.

Educational Boundary

The Citizen's Record is an educational and organizational resource. Nothing on this site is legal advice, and using it does not create an attorney-client relationship. Templates and examples are intended to demonstrate research and record-keeping methods, not to recommend a legal strategy or tell a person what filing they should make.
